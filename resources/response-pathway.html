Plan Overview

Four Principles
Hold It Together

1
Fewer Words As Escalation Rises

As a situation intensifies, the adult says less and focuses on safety and regulation.

2
Correct The Routine, Not The Child

Re-teach the expectation. Never label or shame the student.

3
Repair After Regulation

Address harm through restorative conversation — only once the student is calm.

4
Document & Reflect

Capture what happened and your response. Data drives support, not punishment.

Step 2 — Regulate, In Full

A Break Is A Tool:
Break · Reset · Return

Break

Offer a calm, neutral spot. Invite with a non-verbal cue when you can.

Reset

Stay nearby. Don't lecture. Let them breathe, drink water, use a calming tool.

Return

Bring them back with dignity. A break with no return is exclusion, not regulation.

Say It Like This

"It looks like you need a break. Let's step aside so you can reset and return."

Artifact · Responsive Behavior Plan SOP10

Adults Regulate Too

Model It Out Loud

Pause & Breathe

"I need a moment to take a breath. Let's all take three deep breaths together."

Acknowledge Emotion

"I'm feeling frustrated because we went off track. I'm going to reset and try again."

Restate Expectation

"We correct the routine, not the person. We're going to restart this move and do it right."

Model Repair

"I'm sorry I raised my voice. Next time I'll pause and speak calmly. Thank you for your patience."

Repair & Re-Entry

After Regulation,
Never Before

1RegulateEnsure the student is calm. Breathing, movement, water before anything else.
2RelateBuild connection. "I can see you were frustrated." Listen actively, no fixing.
3Reason"What happened before? Who was affected? What can we do to make it right?"

Repair is specific and dignified — never a forced apology. Re-entry returns the student to the group with belonging intact.

Private Check-In Protocol

The Follow-Up Is
Part Of The Plan

  • Right time, private setting — once calm and the class is working.
  • Affirm presence first — not "What was that about?"
  • Invite the story; validate — silence is valid.
  • Close with dignity — and document the follow-up.
Open With

"I'm glad you're here with us."

Invite

"Would you like to tell me what you were feeling when you needed a break?"

Close

"Thank you for trusting me. I know you can do this."
